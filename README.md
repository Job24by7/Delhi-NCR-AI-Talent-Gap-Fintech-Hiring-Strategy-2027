## Where Fintech Hiring Breaks in Delhi NCR

Look closely at stalled fintech searches in Noida and Gurugram, and the same failures repeat. Credit-risk modellers, fraud analysts, and MLOps engineers are scarce, which invites inflated profiles. Candidates who join for a bigger title leave within a year when the real scope disappoints, and that churn quietly erodes team knowledge. Three costs deserve attention:

- Unverified candidates, where “built a model” means a certification project rather than production work
- Lengthy hiring cycles, where a strong candidate accepts another offer during round five
- Operational cost, where an empty seat or a mis-hire delays compliance, analytics and product work

Turnover deserves its own mention. Fintech candidates in this segment field several calls a week, so a counter-offer is the norm rather than the exception. A recruiter who has already discussed notice periods, growth expectations and past exits can flag flight risk before the offer letter, not after the resignation.

None of these is solved by posting louder. They are solved by checking earlier and deciding faster.

### Q3. Do Delhi NCR fintechs need to pay more than Bengaluru for AI talent?

Not necessarily more, but they must be competitive. Advanced-skill hiring clusters in Tier I metros, so candidates compare offers across cities. ### Q4. Should fintech companies hire experienced professionals or reskill existing staff?

Do both, but sequence them. Experienced hires fill urgent seats such as credit-risk modelling or fraud analytics, while reskilling builds depth over time. Quess Corp’s leadership points to specialist hiring combined with internal mobility as the practical path as centres widen their responsibilities.
